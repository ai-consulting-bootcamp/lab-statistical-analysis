# Statistical-analysis reflection

**What surprised me?** The significance narrative depends strongly on what constitutes a repeat observation. Aggregating to channel-days avoids naively treating many overlapping campaign records as independent observations, but it does not eliminate temporal correlation or audience overlap. Aggregate CPA and the mean of daily CPA also answer different questions; a high-volume day weighs more in the former.

**What changed after correction?** With seven channels, each outcome creates 21 pairwise comparisons. At an uncorrected 5% level, 21 all-null tests would yield about 1.05 false positives *in expectation* (under valid tests); Bonferroni protects the familywise error rate, while Benjamini–Hochberg controls false discovery rate under its assumptions. A corrected significant result does not imply causal superiority. Adjustment must be declared in advance, especially when multiple outcome families are tested.

**Limitations and how I would present them:** The downloaded source is explicitly synthetic, not a client's raw ad history, and its license is unknown. Sales attribution, deduplication, product margin, diminishing returns, seasonality and incrementality are absent. I would tell the CMO that the allocation table is a conservative **scenario**, not a forecast, and propose a geo/audience randomized holdout and an explicit minimum detectable incremental profit threshold before changing the $500K production budget. The power curve assumes independent daily CPA with 15% CV; it should be recalibrated with real dispersion and a clustered trial design.
