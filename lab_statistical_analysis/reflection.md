# Reflection

The most surprising result was that the aggregate CPA estimates were close to one another while Fisher exact tests found many statistically significant conversion-rate differences. The large sample makes tiny percentage-point differences detectable, so statistical significance should not be interpreted as commercial significance.

Multiple-comparison correction removed the small practical CPA conclusions entirely: all 15 CPA comparisons were non-significant both before and after correction. FDR preserved nine of 15 conversion-rate comparisons, whereas Bonferroni preserved seven. This makes FDR the more useful balance for exploratory budget planning, while Bonferroni may be preferred for a single high-stakes decision.

The main limitations are that conversions and revenue were derived from supplied rates and ROI, the campaign records are observational, and aggregate channel comparisons do not measure incremental lift. External factors such as seasonality and audience targeting are not controlled.

I would communicate the findings to non-technical stakeholders by leading with practical differences and uncertainty rather than p-values: Website has the lowest observed CPA, Facebook has the highest observed ROAS, and Email has the highest observed conversion rate, but CPA differences overlap. I would present the portfolio allocation as a test-and-learn plan, not a guaranteed winner-take-all decision.
